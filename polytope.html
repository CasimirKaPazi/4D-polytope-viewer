8 Cubic Cells

Polytope selection: Choose between the four convex regular 4D polytopes.

How to view: Cross your eyes until the two images merge into one central 3D image.

Decompositions: Ways the active polytope splits into smaller regular polytopes. 24-cell offers triality (3 disjoint 16-cells), 16-cell + tesseract, and 3 overlapping tesseracts. Tesseract offers 2 inscribed 16-cells (alternating vertex parity). 16-cell offers 2 inscribed tetrahedra. The polytope's own edges go gray; the decomposition's groups appear in distinct hues. When groups overlap, vertex colors blend - the 3-tesseract case gives the three secondary colors (yellow, cyan, magenta), each marking a specific pair of tesseracts.

Cell highlighting: Visualizes the 3D cells forming each polytope's boundary. Single cell mode picks one cell when there are many.

3D Projection: Projects the 4D polytope onto a 3D hyperplane (W axis, or (1,1,0,0) for the 24-cell), then rotates the result in 3D. For the 24-cell this shows the decomposition 24 = 2 + 6 + 8 + 8 relative to the chosen axis.

Axes view: Draws the four 4D coordinate axes through the origin (red X, yellow-green Y, cyan Z, purple W).

Lattice tiling: Tesseract, 16-cell, and 24-cell tile 4D space. Shows the center plus 8 face-adjacent neighbors at spacing 2.

Hexachromatic: Stereoscopic 2D color space. Left eye colors by angle in XZ, right eye by angle in YW. The brain fuses them into a unique color per S³ point.

3-Sphere view: Stereographic projection from S³ to R³. Geodesics become circular arcs.

Mouse: Drag in left window for XY/XZ (shift: XZ/XW), in right window for YZ/YW (shift: YW/ZW). Wheel to zoom.
